## Principle
中医认为，分娩后身体处于"虚寒"状态——气血双亏，阳气不足，腠理大开。坐月子的全部逻辑是：在身体自然关合之前，用热、补、静来填补这个虚空。 In TCM, birth depletes both Blood (血) and Qi (气) simultaneously — two separate systems that require different treatments. Blood loss is visible; Qi depletion is invisible but equally serious, manifesting as exhaustion, night sweats, and emotional instability.

## Food
- [[Sesame Oil (麻油)|Sesame oil]] (麻油) warms the uterus, lubricates joints stressed by pregnancy, and contains sesamol — a potent antioxidant that crosses the blood-brain barrier, supporting postnatal cognitive recovery (reducing "mummy brain").
- [[Ginger (Halia Tua · 老姜)|Old ginger]] (老姜) is the cornerstone warming agent: it activates TRPV1 receptors (heat receptors), increases local circulation, and has proven anti-nausea, anti-inflammatory, and galactagogue (milk-boosting) properties.
- [[Red Dates · 红枣 (Chinese Jujube)|Red dates]] (红枣) are high in saponins and polysaccharides that specifically stimulate red blood cell production — the empirical foundation of their blood-building reputation is now biochemically understood.
- Avoiding cold water is not superstition: postpartum joints are genuinely hypermobile due to relaxin hormone. Cold causes vasoconstriction in already-compromised tissue; warm water maintains synovial fluid viscosity.
- Pig trotter in black vinegar (猪脚醋) provides collagen precursors (glycine, proline, hydroxyproline), acetic acid that enhances calcium absorption, and ginger compounds — a scientifically coherent postnatal recovery formula.

## Herbs
- [[Dang Gui (当归)|Dang Gui]] (当归) contains ferulic acid and Z-ligustilide — clinically shown to relax uterine smooth muscle, improve blood flow, and exert oestrogen-like effects that ease postnatal hormonal transition.
- Red dates + longan + wolfberry together create a synergistic iron-folate-antioxidant complex: iron from dates, B12 co-factors from longan, and zeaxanthin/lutein from wolfberry for postpartum eye and neural recovery.
- Avoiding plain cold water: TCM holds that "wind" enters through open pores after sweating; physiologically, sudden temperature changes after exertion cause rapid vasoconstriction that stresses healing tissue.

## Confinement
The full-month (满月, mǎn yuè) and 42-day traditions in Chinese culture were recorded as far back as the Zhou Dynasty (1046–256 BC) in the Liji (Book of Rites). The practices were refined by female healers across generations — a clinical database built from millions of postnatal observations, encoding biological wisdom in ritual form.
