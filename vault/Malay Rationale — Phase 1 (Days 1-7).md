## Principle
The postpartum body is considered "open" (terbuka) — pores, joints, and the womb are vulnerable. The entire pantang system exists to protect this opening from cold, wind, and impurity while tissue heals and hormones rebalance.

## Food
- Warming spices ([[Ginger (Halia Tua · 老姜)|ginger]], [[Kunyit (Turmeric)|turmeric]], galangal) increase blood circulation to the uterus, helping it contract back to size (involusi rahim) and expel lochia safely.
- Avoiding cold or raw food prevents "[[Masuk Angin|masuk angin]]" — wind entering the body — which Malay medicine links to joint pain, bloating, and slow recovery that can persist for decades.
- Iron-rich broths and organ meats replace blood lost during birth; coconut milk provides essential fats for breastmilk fat content.
- Restriction on certain vegetables and fruits stems from humoral medicine: foods classified as "sejuk" (cooling) are believed to slow healing and chill the womb.

## Jamu
- [[Kunyit (Turmeric)|Kunyit]] (turmeric) contains curcumin — a proven anti-inflammatory that reduces uterine swelling and supports liver detoxification of postnatal hormones.
- [[Ginger (Halia Tua · 老姜)|Halia tua]] (old ginger) is a COX-2 inhibitor — natural pain relief — and a circulatory stimulant that drives blood to cold, healing tissue.
- Tapel (herbal paste on abdomen) combines heat with transdermal absorption of tightening herbs; the warmth itself aids uterine contraction.
- [[Urutan (Malay Massage)|Urutan]] (massage) accelerates lymphatic drainage, breaks down adhesions from the birth, and — through the release of oxytocin from touch — directly supports breastfeeding.

## Safety Note
If you're breastfeeding, keep today's jamu minum light and diluted rather than concentrated — a newborn's liver is still learning to clear bilirubin, and strong herbal actives can pass through breastmilk in small amounts. Many families wait until baby's jaundice has fully cleared (often by Week 3) before moving to stronger formulations. External treatments — tapel, param, pilis, mandi herbal, and [[Urutan (Malay Massage)|urutan]] — are absorbed far less than anything you drink, so they're generally considered lower-risk throughout. If baby seems unusually fussy, or you notice stool or skin changes, pause and check with your pediatrician.

## Confinement
The 44-day period maps to the medical concept of the puerperium — the 6 weeks during which the uterus returns to pre-pregnancy size, cervical tissue closes, and estrogen/progesterone levels stabilise. Malay tradition arrived at this number through centuries of observation, not written science.
