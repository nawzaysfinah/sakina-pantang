Mature/old ginger (*Zingiber officinale*) is one of the few ingredients used almost identically by [[Malay Pantang]] and [[Chinese Zuo Yuezi]] — the same root, the same warming logic, arrived at independently by two traditions.

Halia tua is a COX-2 inhibitor — natural pain relief — and a circulatory stimulant that drives blood to cold, healing tissue. In Chinese practice, old ginger (老姜) activates TRPV1 heat receptors, increases local circulation, and has documented anti-nausea, anti-inflammatory, and galactagogue (milk-boosting) properties.

Both traditions avoid cold water after birth for a related reason: postpartum joints are genuinely hypermobile due to relaxin hormone, and cold causes vasoconstriction in already-compromised tissue — warm water (and warm ginger drinks) maintain synovial fluid viscosity.

Used in: [[Malay Rationale — Phase 1 (Days 1-7)]], [[Chinese Rationale — Phase 1 (Days 1-7)]], and as a daily jamu ingredient through much of the 44 days.
