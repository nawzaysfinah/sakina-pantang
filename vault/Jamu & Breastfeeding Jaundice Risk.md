# Jamu & Breastfeeding Jaundice Risk

Neonatal jaundice is extremely common in the first few weeks of life. Active bio-compounds and concentrated botanicals in herbal drinks like [[Kunyit (Turmeric)|jamu]] can pass through breast milk. The newborn liver is still developing and may struggle to process these complex compounds, potentially worsening neonatal jaundice or elevating bilirubin levels.

There's also a standardisation problem: traditional jamu formulations vary widely by practitioner or brand, making exact dosage and purity hard to regulate. Spices like raw ginger, turmeric, and acidic ingredients like tamarind can also cause heartburn or an upset stomach for some new mothers, independent of the jaundice question.

## Practical guidance

- **First 2–3 weeks (breastfeeding):** hold off on concentrated jamu minum until baby's jaundice has been checked and cleared by a pediatrician.
- **Topical treatments are different:** [[Urutan (Malay Massage)|urutan]], tapel, param, pilis, and herbal baths are absorbed far less than anything drunk — generally considered lower-risk throughout.
- **When reintroducing:** start with a small, diluted portion. Watch baby for fussiness, stool changes, or skin reactions.
- **Don't let this replace the basics:** balanced nutrition, plain water, rest, and medical follow-up matter more than any single herbal drink.
